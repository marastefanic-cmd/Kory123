What the benchmark sim is — and what it isn't

It is not a simulation of your character. It never asks for your gear, and it couldn't use it if you gave it to us. It runs the benchmark this project uses to check its own model: a fixed reference mage that has been dialled in over hundreds of verification runs.

What it actually does

It takes two layouts — the planner's, and one alternative — and asks the real wowsims engine one narrow question: which of these two does the simulator prefer? Both sides run the identical character through the identical fights (same random seed), so the difference between them is trustworthy even though neither absolute number describes you.

The benchmark character

  • No gear, no consumables, no raid buffs — your spell damage, crit and haste rating are injected as flat numbers on top of a bare level-70 Arcane mage. They are the only three things about you the planner knows, so they are the only three the sim gets.
  • Spell hit pinned at the cap, so misses never enter the comparison.
  • Infinite mana and no conserve rotation — it casts Arcane Blast start to finish. Real play weaves Frostbolt and drinks gems; that changes your DPS, not which cooldown layout wins.
  • Nothing cast before the pull, and the fight ends within half a second of the length you entered — the same assumptions the planner itself makes.

So how do I read the number?

Read the gap, never the DPS. "+0.4%" means the simulator agrees the plan is better by about that much, on a fight shaped like the one you described. The absolute figures are the benchmark mage's, and will not match your log, your sim, or your guild's spreadsheet — by design.

What it can't see

  • Drums of Battle and Power Infusion. The simulator will not let a rotation script press these two: it builds them as buffs it fires on its own schedule, not as spells a plan can trigger — and a scheduled press is ignored without complaint (we checked: identical damage, no buff applied). Bloodlust is pressable, which is why it works. So both sides run without Drums and PI, and the result panel names whatever it had to drop.
  • The Ashtongue Talisman, for a different reason: it is a random proc, so there is no press to schedule in the first place.
  • Burn phases — "the boss takes ×N damage" is a planner concept with no button in the simulator, so it refuses to guess rather than compare something else.
  • Mana, movement, mistakes, and your actual gear.

Under the hood this is the same engine, the same settings and the same plan transcription this project runs in the terminal to verify the model — wired to a button rather than re-implemented. If it and the planner ever disagree about which layout wins, that disagreement is a finding, and the panel says so.
